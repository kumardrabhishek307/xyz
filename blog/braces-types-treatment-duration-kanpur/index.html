Braces: Types, Treatment Time and How to Care for Them

Crooked teeth, gaps and a misaligned bite are not only a cosmetic concern. They can make teeth harder to clean and put uneven pressure on the jaw. This guide compares the main types of braces and aligners, explains how treatment works and how long it takes, and shows how to look after your teeth during and after treatment.

Written by Dr. Abhishek Pal
BDS (Oral Medicine & Radiology) · 10+ years experience
Updated 20 September 2026 9 min read
Quick answer

Braces gradually move teeth into a better position using gentle, constant pressure. Options include metal braces, ceramic (tooth-coloured) braces and clear aligners. Most treatment takes 12–24 months, followed by retainers to hold the result. The best choice depends on your bite, age, budget and lifestyle, and it is decided after an examination and X-rays.

Do you need braces?

A dentist can tell you for certain, but these are the common reasons people are advised to have orthodontic treatment:

Common alignment problems that braces correct
ProblemWhat it looks likeWhy it matters
Crowding Teeth overlap or twist because there is not enough room Hard to clean, so higher risk of decay and gum problems
Spacing Gaps between teeth Food trapping, gum irritation, appearance concerns
Overjet or protruding front teeth Upper front teeth stick out Higher chance of injury, difficulty closing the lips
Deep bite (overbite) Upper teeth cover the lower teeth too much Excess wear, and lower teeth can bite into the gum
Underbite Lower front teeth sit in front of the upper teeth Chewing difficulty and uneven wear
Crossbite Some upper teeth bite inside the lower teeth Uneven wear, and it may push the jaw to one side
Open bite Front teeth do not meet when the back teeth close Difficulty biting food, and it can affect speech

Some jaw-size problems in growing children, or severe skeletal differences in adults, may need a specialist orthodontist or a combined approach. A good dentist will tell you honestly if referral is the better option.

Fees vary with the case, the brand and how long treatment takes, so a reliable quote is only possible after your examination. Ask for a written plan that states the total treatment cost, the number of visits and what is included.

The right age to start

  • Around age 7: A first check-up is recommended to spot jaw growth or habit problems (such as thumb sucking) early. Most children do not need braces yet.
  • Ages 11–14: Most active braces treatment begins once the permanent teeth have come in, while the jaws are still growing.
  • Teenagers and adults: Treatment works at any age if the gums and bone supporting the teeth are healthy. Gum disease and decay should be treated first.

How treatment works

  1. Consultation and records The dentist examines your teeth, bite and gums, takes photographs and X-rays (usually an OPG, sometimes a side-view skull X-ray) and may take impressions or digital scans.
  2. Treatment plan You are shown the problem, the options, the expected time, the number of visits and the full cost, before anything is started.
  3. Preparation Cavities, gum problems and a professional cleaning are done first. In some cases, teeth are removed or space is created to make room.
  4. Placing braces or first aligners Brackets are bonded to the teeth and a thin wire is fitted, or the first set of aligners is issued. Fitting is not painful.
  5. Regular adjustments Visits every 4 to 8 weeks change wires or elastics. Aligner wearers switch to new trays as advised and are reviewed regularly.
  6. Finishing and retention Braces are removed, teeth are polished and retainers are fitted to hold the new position.

How long does treatment take?

Typical treatment time by case
CaseUsual time with bracesNotes
Mild (small gaps or slight crowding) About 6–12 months Aligners are often suitable
Moderate crowding or bite issues About 12–24 months The most common range
Complex (severe crowding, extractions, jaw mismatch) About 24–36 months May need extra appliances or specialist input

These are general ranges. Missing appointments, broken brackets, poor cleaning and not wearing elastics or aligners as advised are the most common reasons treatment runs longer.

Caring for your braces

Food and plaque collect easily around brackets. Poor cleaning can leave permanent white marks on the enamel and cause swollen gums, so home care matters as much as the appointments.

Do

  • Brush after every meal and before bed, using a soft brush and fluoride toothpaste.
  • Clean around each bracket, and along the gum line, angling the brush up and down.
  • Use an interdental brush, floss threaders or a water flosser once a day.
  • Rinse with water after eating when you cannot brush.
  • Eat soft, cut-up food for the first few days after fitting and after adjustments.
  • Wear your elastics or aligners exactly as you are told.
  • Keep a small kit with a brush, wax and a mirror in your bag.

Don't

  • Avoid hard and sticky foods such as chikki, toffee, chewing gum, ice and hard nuts.
  • Do not bite directly into whole apples, corn cobs or sugarcane. Cut them into pieces.
  • Do not chew pens, nails or straws.
  • Cut back on sweets and sugary drinks, because sugar plus plaque causes cavities.
  • Do not bend wires or remove brackets yourself.
  • Avoid tobacco, paan and gutkha. They stain teeth and harm gums.
  • Do not skip appointments, even if you feel fine.

Wire or bracket rubbing the cheek Press orthodontic wax over the spot The wire is sharp or keeps cutting the cheek

Seek urgent care if:

You have facial swelling, fever, severe pain, or an injury to your mouth or teeth (for example, a blow that cuts the lips or gums). If a piece of braces is swallowed and you have difficulty breathing or severe pain, go to a hospital emergency straight away.

After braces: retainers

Teeth tend to drift back toward their old positions after braces come off, because the ligaments and bone around them need time to stabilise. Retainers prevent this "relapse". They can be a removable plastic or wire retainer, or a thin fixed wire bonded behind the front teeth.

  • Wear them for as long and as often as advised, which is usually full-time at first and then at night.
  • Keep fixed retainers clean with floss threaders or an interdental brush.
  • Store removable retainers in their case, away from heat and pets.
  • Bring a broken or loose retainer for repair straight away.

Frequently asked questions

Are braces painful?

Braces are not painful during fitting, but teeth feel sore and tender for about 2 to 5 days after the braces are placed and after some adjustments. Soft food, warm salt-water rinses and orthodontic wax for rubbing wires usually make this manageable.

How long do I need to wear braces?

Most treatments take 12 to 24 months, though mild cases can be shorter and complex cases longer. Regular visits, good oral hygiene and following the dentist's instructions on elastics and diet help you finish on time. After braces, retainers are needed to keep teeth in position.

Can adults get braces?

Yes. Braces and aligners work at any age as long as the teeth, gums and supporting bone are healthy. Gum disease and decay should be treated before orthodontic treatment begins.

Are clear aligners better than braces?

Neither is better for everyone. Aligners are nearly invisible and removable, but they need to be worn 20 to 22 hours a day and suit mild to moderate problems best. Fixed braces work continuously and can handle more complex tooth movements. Your dentist will recommend the option that suits your teeth and lifestyle.

Will I need to have teeth removed for braces?

Not always. Extractions are sometimes needed to create space when teeth are severely crowded or protruding, but many cases are treated without removing any teeth. The decision is made after examination, photographs, X-rays and a study of your bite.
